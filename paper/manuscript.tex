\title{Systematic Comparison of Imbalance Mitigation Strategies for Ordinal Medical Image Classification: Adjacent Mixup, Ordinal-Weighted Mixup, and Balanced Softmax on Knee OA Grading}

\titlerunning{Imbalance Mitigation for Knee OA Grading}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Class imbalance and ordinality are two intertwined challenges in medical image classification.
Knee Osteoarthritis (OA) severity grading---a 5-class ordinal task---suffers from severe class imbalance,
where the intermediate Grade~1 (Doubtful) is systematically underdiagnosed.
We present a systematic comparison of 10 imbalance mitigation strategies spanning re-weighting,
margin-based, and data augmentation approaches, evaluated on the Chen~2018 Knee OA dataset.
Our study comprises 18 experimental configurations, including standard baselines (CE, Focal Loss,
Balanced Softmax), input-level mixup variants (Vanilla Mixup, Adjacent Mixup, Rule-based Mixup,
SupCon), and a proposed Ordinal-Weighted Mixup (OWMix) that incorporates a Gaussian kernel
weighted by ordinal distance.
We find that Adjacent Mixup combined with Balanced Softmax achieves the best overall
performance (F1=0.699, QWK=0.843, MAE=0.373), while OWMix with Balanced Softmax ($\tau$=2.0)
achieves competitive ordinal metrics (QWK=0.843, MAE=0.368).
Despite these gains, Grade~1 remains a persistent bottleneck (F1~0.38--0.41 across all methods),
highlighting the need for dedicated ordinal-sensitive architectures.

\keywords{Medical Image Classification, Class Imbalance, Ordinal Classification, Mixup, Balanced Softmax, Knee Osteoarthritis}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Knee Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disease affecting over 500 million people worldwide.
Radiographic assessment using the Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grading system is the clinical standard,
assigning a severity grade from 0 (Healthy) to 4 (Severe).
Automated KL grading is an ordinal classification problem where grades follow a natural
ordering, and misclassifications incur asymmetric costs---predicting Grade~0 for a Grade~4 knee
is far more dangerous than predicting Grade~3.

Three major challenges complicate automated KL grading:
(i) Severe class imbalance: Grade~0 dominates while Grade~4 is scarce;
(ii) Ordinal ambiguity: Grade~1 (Doubtful) is inherently subjective and poorly separated
from adjacent grades, leading to consistently low recall;
(iii) Overfitting: Small dataset size (~3,500 training images) makes deep models prone to
memorization.

Prior work addresses these challenges individually through re-weighting~\cite{lin2017focal},
margin-based losses~\cite{ren2020balanced}, and data augmentation~\cite{zhang2018mixup},
but a systematic comparison across method families on the same medical ordinal dataset is lacking.
Furthermore, while feature-level ordinal mixup has been explored~\cite{li2025dfpg},
input-level ordinal-aware mixup remains understudied.

This study bridges this gap by:
\begin{itemize}
\item benchmarking 10 imbalance strategies (18 configurations) on a unified pipeline
  with fixed architecture (ResNet-50), training protocol, and evaluation;
\item proposing Ordinal-Weighted Mixup (OWMix), a Gaussian-kernel-based mixup
  that assigns higher mixing coefficients to samples from adjacent ordinal classes;
\item identifying Adjacent Mixup + Balanced Softmax as the best-performing combination
  (F1=0.699, QWK=0.843, MAE=0.373), improving 5.2\% F1 over the CE baseline;
\item revealing the persistent Grade~1 bottleneck as a fundamental limitation of
  post-hoc correction methods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Background: Imbalance in Ordinal Classification}

Class imbalance in medical image classification is commonly addressed through three families
of methods: re-weighting, margin-based losses, and data augmentation.

\textbf{Focal Loss}~\cite{lin2017focal} down-weights easy samples using a modulating factor
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{FL}} = -\sum_k (1-p_k)^\gamma \log p_k$ with $\gamma=2.0$.

\textbf{Balanced Softmax}~\cite{ren2020balanced} adjusts logits by incorporating prior
class frequencies. Given sample count $n_k$ for class $k$, the adjusted probability is:
\begin{equation}
   p_k' = \frac{n_k e^{z_k}}{\sum_j n_j e^{z_j}},
\end{equation}
effectively performing label-distribution-aware margin scaling.

\textbf{Vanilla Mixup}~\cite{zhang2018mixup} creates convex combinations of random sample pairs:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \lambda \mathbf{x}_i + (1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_j,\quad
\tilde{y} = \lambda y_i + (1-\lambda) y_j,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \alpha)$, $\alpha=0.4$.

\textbf{Adjacent Mixup} restricts mixing to pairs where $|y_i - y_j| \leq 1$, preserving
ordinal locality. \textbf{Rule-based Mixup} uses a fixed $\lambda=0.49$ when mixing
Grade~0 with other classes to prevent dominant class dilution.

\textbf{SupCon}~\cite{khosla2020supervised} uses supervised contrastive learning in
a 3-phase pipeline: pretrain with SupCon loss, linear probe, then full finetune.

\subsection{Ordinal-Weighted Mixup (OWMix)}

We propose OWMix, which assigns mixing weights via a Gaussian kernel over ordinal distance:
\begin{equation}
w(y_i, y_j) = \exp\left(-\frac{(y_i - y_j)^2}{2\tau^2}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is a temperature hyperparameter controlling kernel width.
The mixing coefficient is then:
\begin{equation}
\lambda' = w(y_i, y_j) \cdot \lambda + (1 - w(y_i, y_j)) \cdot 0.5,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \alpha)$.
This formulation smoothly interpolates between standard mixup (when $\tau \to \infty$)
and near-adjacent-only mixing (when $\tau \to 0$).
We sweep $\tau \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$ for both CE and Balanced Softmax variants.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Dataset}

We use the Chen~2018 Knee OA dataset~\cite{chen2018knee}, consisting of 4,760 PA knee
radiographs with KL grades 0--4.
The official split allocates ~75\% for training (3,522 images) and ~25\% for testing (1,238 images).
Following prior work, we further split the training set into train (90\%) and validation (10\%)
stratified by class.
All images are resized to $224\times224$, normalized with ImageNet statistics, and augmented
with random horizontal flip and rotation $\pm 10^\circ$.

Table~\ref{tab:dataset} shows the class distribution.
Grade~0 is the most frequent class, while Grade~4 is the rarest, creating a long-tailed

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

All experiments use a ResNet-50~\cite{he2016deep} pretrained on ImageNet,
with the final layer replaced by a 5-way classifier. Input size is $224\times224$.
We train for 30 epochs with batch size 32, Adam optimizer ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$),
learning rate $10^{-4}$, and weight decay $10^{-4}$.
Early stopping with patience 7 on validation F1-macro is applied.
All runs use a fixed seed 42 for reproducibility.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We report F1-macro (primary), Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK), Mean Absolute Error (MAE),
accuracy, and per-class F1 scores.
QWK and MAE capture ordinal misclassification severity beyond accuracy.

F1-macro is the unweighted mean of per-class F1 scores:
\begin{equation}
   \text{F1-macro} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision}_k \cdot \text{Recall}_k}{\text{Precision}_k + \text{Recall}_k}
\end{equation}

Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) measures agreement between predictions and ground truth,
with quadratic penalty for ordinal deviations:
\begin{equation}
   \kappa = 1 - \frac{\sum_i \sum_j w_{ij} O_{ij}}{\sum_i \sum_j w_{ij} E_{ij}},
\end{equation}
where $w_{ij} = (i-j)^2$, $O$ is the observation matrix, and $E$ is the expected matrix
under random chance.

Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in the ordinal context measures the average absolute grade deviation:
\begin{equation}
   \text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |\hat{y}_i - y_i|
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:model-result} presents the full comparison across all 18 configurations.
Several key findings emerge.

\textbf{Adjacent Mixup + Balanced Softmax (v7) achieves the best overall F1.}
v7 achieves F1=0.699, QWK=0.843, and MAE=0.373, outperforming all other methods on the
primary F1 metric. The combination of ordinal-aware mixing (restricting to adjacent classes)
and label-distribution-aware logit adjustment provides complementary benefits.

\textbf{OWMix with Balanced Softmax is competitive on ordinal metrics.}
v12d (OWMix $\tau$=2.0 + BalSoft) achieves QWK=0.843 (tying v7) and the best MAE
among BalSoft variants (0.368), while v11b (OWMix $\tau$=1.0, CE) achieves the
overall best MAE of 0.364. This suggests that Gaussian-weighted mixing with a wide
kernel ($\tau=2.0$) provides ordinal awareness without overly restricting the mixing pool.

\textbf{Balanced Softmax consistently improves CE.}
Across all four mixup variants (Vanilla, Adjacent, Rule, OWMix), replacing CE with BalSoft
improves F1 by 1--3 points, confirming its effectiveness as a complementary strategy.

\textbf{Temperature ablation.}
For CE variants, $\tau=1.0$ is optimal. For BalSoft variants, $\tau=2.0$ is best.
BalSoft already provides class-aware logit adjustment, reducing the need for aggressive
ordinal weighting---hence a wider kernel is preferred.
Overall, temperature has modest impact (F1 range 0.666--0.689 for CE, 0.678--0.685 for BalSoft).

\textbf{The Grade~1 bottleneck.}
Grade~1 (Doubtful) F1 ranges from 0.282 (CE baseline) to a maximum of 0.413---the lowest
across all five classes. Even v7 (+5.2\% F1 over baseline) only improves Grade~1 from
0.282 to 0.413. This is consistent with clinical reality: Grade~1 is defined as doubtful,
and its boundaries with Grade~0 and Grade~2 are inherently ambiguous.
Post-hoc corrections appear fundamentally limited for high-ambiguity ordinal categories.

\section{Conclusion}

We systematically compared 18 imbalance mitigation configurations for ordinal medical image
classification on the Chen~2018 Knee OA dataset.
Adjacent Mixup + Balanced Softmax achieves the best overall performance (F1=0.699, QWK=0.843),
while OWMix provides competitive results on ordinal metrics.
The persistent Grade~1 bottleneck (maximum F1=0.413) indicates that post-hoc corrections
are insufficient for high-ambiguity ordinal categories.
Future work should investigate explicit ordinal constraints, uncertainty quantification,
and multi-dataset validation.
