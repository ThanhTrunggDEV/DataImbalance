\title{OWMM: Ordinal-Weighted Manifold Mixup with a Tempered Prior for Imbalanced Medical Image Grading}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Medical severity grading---such as knee osteoarthritis and diabetic retinopathy---is simultaneously ordinal and severely class-imbalanced, a combination that defeats generic imbalance remedies. Loss re-weighting corrects frequency but ignores grade ordering, while mixup augmentation blends images in pixel space, an operation that destroys the fine-grained texture on which medical decisions rely and treats a Grade~0/Grade~4 blend as no less valid than a Grade~0/Grade~1 one. We propose Ordinal-Weighted Manifold Mixup: mixing partners are sampled by a Gaussian kernel over ordinal distance modulated by an effective-number frequency weight, the interpolation is performed on hidden features rather than pixels, and supervision uses a soft target smoothed along the grade axis. Crucially, the method couples this with a tempered class prior $\gamma\in[0,1]$ that continuously interpolates between plain cross-entropy ($\gamma=0$) and full Balanced Softmax ($\gamma=1$). On two five-grade benchmarks---knee osteoarthritis radiographs and EyePACS retinal fundus images---it is the only compared method that surpasses the cross-entropy baseline on both datasets across macro F1, quadratic weighted kappa, and mean absolute error. An ablation shows why the tempered prior is essential: full Balanced Softmax collapses the majority grade on the highly skewed retinopathy data (macro F1 $0.278$), while an intermediate $\gamma$ recovers a robust optimum on both datasets, with the optimal $\gamma$ tracking imbalance severity.

\keywords{Medical Image Grading, Class Imbalance, Manifold Mixup, Ordinal Classification, Knee Osteoarthritis, Diabetic Retinopathy}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Medical image grading tasks such as Knee Osteoarthritis (OA) severity on the Kellgren--Lawrence (KL) system~\cite{kellgren1957radiological} and Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) grading~\cite{gulshan2016development} share two challenges: they are \textit{ordinal} (grades progress from healthy to severe) and highly imbalanced (healthy cases vastly outnumber severe ones)~\cite{buda2018systematic}. Standard cross-entropy (CE) is ill-suited: it maximizes overall accuracy by defaulting to the majority grade, leaving the rare intermediate grades---often the clinically decisive ones---poorly recognized, a skew-driven failure documented well beyond medicine~\cite{phung2026xai}, while ignoring the ordinal structure that could guide generalization.

A large body of work addresses imbalance through loss-based corrections. Focal Loss~\cite{lin2017focal} down-weights easy samples; Balanced Softmax~\cite{ren2020balanced} shifts logits by a log-frequency prior, with a smoothed variant for extreme imbalance~\cite{vu2024smooth}; logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021logit} scales that prior; and Class-Balanced loss~\cite{cui2019classbalanced} re-weights by the effective number of samples. Self-labeling refines tail classes without extra supervision~\cite{vu2024lcsl}. Augmentation is orthogonal: Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup} combines training pairs, with variants on hidden representations~\cite{verma2019manifold}, salient regions~\cite{yun2019cutmix}, class-balanced sampling~\cite{galdran2021balanced}, or soft labels distilled from augmented views~\cite{thuong2025skdnet}, and long-tailed analyses~\cite{vu2024augmentation}. For ordinal structure, OR-CNN~\cite{niu2016coral} encodes grade order via cumulative binary subtasks, and---closest to us---ordinal label-distribution learning with asymmetric priors~\cite{shaik2025ordinal} targets imbalanced retinal grading.

These leave a gap on ordinal, imbalanced, fine-grained images. First, mixup variants interpolate in pixel space: alpha-blending two fundus photographs or knee radiographs is anatomically meaningless and erases the subtle cues (osteophytes, micro-aneurysms) grading depends on, so the signal is largely noise. Second, pairing is ordinal-blind---a healthy/severe blend is as likely as a healthy/doubtful one. Third, loss re-weighting is binary: CE applies none, while full Balanced Softmax applies the entire prior and, under extreme imbalance, over-corrects and abandons the majority. No single configuration is robust across regimes.

In this paper we propose Ordinal-Weighted Manifold Mixup (OWMM), which (i) samples partners with a Gaussian kernel over ordinal grade distance modulated by an effective-number frequency weight, (ii) interpolates on intermediate feature maps where linear combinations are semantically valid, preserving pixel-level texture, (iii) supervises with an ordinally smoothed soft target, and (iv) replaces the binary loss choice with a tempered prior $\gamma\in[0,1]$ bridging CE and Balanced Softmax. Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item OWMM, a mixup that interpolates ordinal-selected partners on the feature manifold, coupled with a continuous tempered class prior $\gamma$;
  \item Evidence that OWMM is the only compared method beating CE on both KOA and EyePACS across macro F1, quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) and mean absolute error (MAE);
  \item An ablation showing the tempered prior is necessary---full Balanced Softmax collapses the majority grade under severe imbalance---and that the optimal $\gamma$ tracks imbalance severity.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Setup}

We cast ordinal medical grading as a $K$-class classification problem ($K=5$). Given a training set $\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ of images $\mathbf{x}_i$ and ordinal labels $y_i\in\{0,\dots,K\!-\!1\}$ ordered from healthy ($0$) to severe ($K\!-\!1$), we learn a model $f_\theta$ with a backbone feature extractor and a linear head producing logits $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}$. Two properties make the task hard. (i)~\textit{Imbalance}: the per-class training counts $n_0,\dots,n_{K-1}$ (with $\sum_{c} n_c = N$) are highly skewed ($n_0\gg n_{K-1}$; $2{,}286$ vs.\ $173$ on KOA, a ratio of $\sim\!13\times$, and up to $\sim\!36\times$ on EyePACS), so a model minimizing average error collapses onto the majority grade. (ii)~\textit{Ordinality}: grades lie on a line, so confusing adjacent grades is far less costly than confusing distant ones---a structure CE ignores. Writing $B$ for the mini-batch size and $\mathbf{e}_c$ for the one-hot vector of class $c$, our objective is an augmentation that exploits (ii) to mitigate (i): raising minority-grade recognition without sacrificing the majority, and respecting grade order. Accordingly we evaluate both frequency-aware (macro F1) and ordinal criteria (QWK, MAE) rather than accuracy alone.

\subsection{Ordinal-Weighted Manifold Mixup}

OWMM augments each mini-batch through four coupled mechanisms; Fig.~\ref{fig:method} illustrates the pipeline and Alg.~\ref{alg:owmm} gives the procedure.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/pipeline_owmm}
\caption{OWMM pipeline. Partners are selected by an ordinal Gaussian kernel with effective-number frequency weighting; interpolation is applied to the \texttt{layer3} feature manifold; the head is supervised with an ordinally smoothed soft target; and a tempered prior $\gamma$ scales the Balanced-Softmax logit shift.}
\label{fig:method}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Ordinal-weighted partner selection.} For an anchor $i$ we sample a partner $j$ with probability proportional to a Gaussian kernel over ordinal distance, modulated by the partner class's frequency weight:
\begin{equation}
p_{ij}\ \propto\ \exp\!\left(-\frac{(y_i-y_j)^2}{\tau^2}\right)\cdot w_{y_j},\qquad p_{ii}=0,
\label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ controls how sharply the weight decays with grade distance (fixed to $\tau=1$). The frequency weight uses the \textit{effective number} of samples~\cite{cui2019classbalanced}, $w_c=(1-\beta)/(1-\beta^{\,n_c})$ (mean-normalized, $\beta=0.9999$), up-weighting rare grades far more aggressively than inverse-square-root weighting---necessary at $36\times$ imbalance. A partner is drawn as $j\sim\text{Multinomial}(p_{i1},\dots,p_{iB})$.

\textbf{Manifold interpolation.} Rather than blending pixels, we forward each image $\mathbf{x}_i$ through the backbone $f_\theta$ to an intermediate stage (ResNet \texttt{layer3}) and mix the feature maps $\mathbf{h}_i,\mathbf{h}_j$ of the anchor and its partner,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}=\lambda_i\,\mathbf{h}_i+(1-\lambda_i)\,\mathbf{h}_{j},\qquad \lambda_i\sim\text{Beta}(\alpha,\alpha),
\label{eq:manifold}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=0.4$ and $\lambda_i$ drawn per anchor; $\mathbf{h}$ then continues through the remaining layers. Feature-space interpolation is semantically valid and leaves the input texture intact~\cite{verma2019manifold}. A scarcity-adaptive scale then shrinks the partner fraction for majority anchors, $\lambda_i \leftarrow 1-(1-\lambda_i)\,s_{y_i}$ with $s_c=\operatorname{clip}(w_c,\,s_{\min},\,1)$ and $s_{\min}=0.3$, preserving full mixing strength for rare anchors while protecting majority accuracy under extreme skew.

\textbf{Soft ordinal target.} The mixed sample is supervised with a two-hot target $\lambda_i\,\mathbf{e}_{y_i}+(1-\lambda_i)\,\mathbf{e}_{y_j}$ additionally smoothed by a Gaussian over the grade axis (width $\sigma=0.5$), training against a distribution consistent with ordinal geometry rather than a hard label.

\textbf{Tempered prior.} We generalize Balanced Softmax~\cite{ren2020balanced} with a temper $\gamma\in[0,1]$ on the log-frequency prior:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{CE}\big(\mathbf{z}+\gamma\log\mathbf{n},\ \tilde{\mathbf{y}}\big),
\label{eq:temper}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{n}=(n_0,\dots,n_{K-1})$ gathers the per-class counts of the training set $\mathcal{D}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}$ is the soft target. Here $\gamma=0$ recovers CE, $\gamma=1$ is full Balanced Softmax, and intermediate $\gamma$ applies a partial correction (equivalent to scaled logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021logit}). We do not additionally apply class-balanced resampling, so the partner weighting of Eq.~\ref{eq:pair} and the tempered prior of Eq.~\ref{eq:temper} are the only balancing mechanisms, avoiding double correction.

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
\DontPrintSemicolon
\caption{OWMM training step on a mini-batch}
\label{alg:owmm}
\KwIn{$\{(\mathbf{x}_i,y_i)\}_{i=1}^{B}$;\ model $f_\theta$;\ counts $\mathbf{n}$;\ weights $\{w_c\}$;\ scales $\{s_c\}$;\ $\gamma,\alpha,\tau,\sigma$}
\KwOut{updated parameters $\theta$}
$\mathbf{h}_{1:B} \leftarrow f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{1:B})$ up to \texttt{layer3}\;
\For{$i \leftarrow 1$ \KwTo $B$}{
  $\lambda_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\alpha)$;\quad $\lambda_i \leftarrow 1-(1-\lambda_i)\,s_{y_i}$\tcp*{scarcity-adaptive strength}
  $j \sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(p_{i\cdot})$,\ \ $p_{ij}\propto e^{-(y_i-y_j)^2/\tau^2}w_{y_j}$\tcp*{Eq.~\ref{eq:pair}}
  $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i \leftarrow \lambda_i\mathbf{h}_i + (1-\lambda_i)\mathbf{h}_j$;\quad $\mathbf{z}_i \leftarrow \mathrm{Head}(\mathrm{Layer4}(\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i))$\tcp*{Eq.~\ref{eq:manifold}}
  $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i \leftarrow \mathrm{OrdSmooth}(\lambda_i\mathbf{e}_{y_i} + (1-\lambda_i)\mathbf{e}_{y_j};\ \sigma)$\;
}
$\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \tfrac{1}{B}\sum_i \mathrm{CE}(\mathbf{z}_i + \gamma\log\mathbf{n},\ \tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i)$;\quad $\theta \leftarrow \mathrm{AdamW}(\theta, \nabla_\theta\mathcal{L})$\tcp*{Eq.~\ref{eq:temper}}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Relation to prior work.} With pixel-space mixing, a hard neighbor threshold, no soft-target smoothing, and $\gamma=1$, OWMM reduces to ordinal/adjacent mixup plus Balanced Softmax; with $\tau\!\to\!\infty$ and $\gamma=0$ it reduces to vanilla Mixup~\cite{zhang2018mixup}. OWMM's novelty is the combination of manifold interpolation, ordinal partner selection with effective-number weighting, and the continuous tempered prior.

\section{Experiment}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We compare six configurations (Table~\ref{tab:config}): a CE baseline; two loss corrections (Balanced Softmax, Focal Loss with focusing parameter $2$); vanilla Mixup; Adjacent Mixup (hard neighbor threshold with Balanced Softmax); and our OWMM, whose temper $\gamma$ is selected by validation macro F1 per dataset. As these differ along several axes, the controlled ablation is the $\gamma$-sweep of Table~\ref{tab:gamma}. All models use an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50~\cite{he2016deep} with a Dropout($0.5$)+linear head on the $2048$-d features. We train $30$ epochs (batch $32$) with AdamW (lr $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), halving the lr on validation-loss plateau (patience $3$, factor $0.5$), clipping gradients to norm $1.0$, and early-stopping (patience $7$) on validation macro F1. For OWMM we fix $\tau=1$, $\alpha=0.4$, effective-number $\beta=0.9999$, ordinal smoothing $\sigma=0.5$, and majority-protection floor $s_{\min}=0.3$. All results use seed $123$, trained in PyTorch on one NVIDIA RTX A4000 ($16$ GB).

\subsection{Datasets}

We use two medical benchmarks differing in scale, modality, and imbalance structure.

\textbf{Knee OA}~\cite{kaggle_koa} consists of 8{,}260 PA knee radiographs graded KL~0--4 (denoted G0--G4), split into 5{,}778 training, 826 validation, and 1{,}656 test images. Grade~0 (Healthy) dominates (2{,}286 training samples) while Grade~4 (Severe) has only 173 (imbalance ratio $\sim\!13\times$); Grade~1 (Doubtful) is particularly challenging due to its inherent ambiguity.

\textbf{EyePACS DR}~\cite{kaggle_dr,gulshan2016development} contains 35{,}108 retinal fundus images, split via stratified 70/10/20 into 24{,}575 training, 3{,}511 validation, and 7{,}022 test. Grade~0 (No DR) dominates the training set (18{,}061 samples) while DR~4 is rarest (496), an imbalance ratio of $\sim\!36\times$. This dataset tests whether ordinal-aware methods generalize to larger, noisier, more severely imbalanced tasks.

All images are resized to $224\times224$ with ImageNet normalization. Training augmentations include random resized crop, random horizontal flip, $\pm10^\circ$ rotation, and color jitter (brightness/contrast $\pm0.2$).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We report four complementary metrics. Per-class F1 exposes the disparities that aggregate scores can mask on imbalanced ordinal data. Macro F1 (the unweighted mean of per-class F1) is our primary frequency-aware criterion. QWK~\cite{cohen1968weighted} captures ordinal consistency by penalizing predictions proportionally to their squared distance from the true grade, and MAE measures the average grade distance; both are clinically meaningful for graded scales.

\textbf{Limitations of the CE baseline.} The CE baseline reaches macro F1~=~0.664, but this masks severe per-class disparity: Grade~1 (Doubtful) languishes at 0.370 while Grade~4 (Severe) reaches 0.791. The baseline has no mechanism to redistribute capacity toward ambiguous intermediate grades.

\textbf{Partial effectiveness of loss re-weighting.} Balanced Softmax lifts Grade~1 to 0.408 and Grade~4 to 0.854 (macro F1~=~0.687), but Focal Loss harms the majority (Grade~0 drops to 0.679) and lowers QWK to 0.801, showing that aggressive re-weighting alone trades majority performance for minority gains.

\textbf{Impact of ordinal manifold mixing.} OWMM delivers the best aggregate results---macro F1~=~0.697, QWK~=~0.836 and the lowest MAE~=~0.371---while lifting the difficult mid-grade Grade~2 from 0.570 to 0.635. OWMM also edges out pixel-space Adjacent Mixup (macro F1 0.686, QWK 0.829). Crucially, Grade~0 is only marginally affected ($-0.012$), confirming that ordinal-aware manifold mixing with a tempered prior redistributes rather than sacrifices learning capacity.

\section{Conclusion}

We proposed OWMM, an ordinal-weighted manifold mixup paired with a tempered class prior for imbalanced medical grading. By mixing ordinal-selected partners in feature space rather than pixels, and by replacing the binary CE/Balanced-Softmax choice with a continuous temper $\gamma$, OWMM is the only compared method to surpass the CE baseline on both Knee OA and EyePACS across macro F1, QWK and MAE. Our ablation shows the tempered prior is essential---full re-weighting collapses the majority grade under severe imbalance---and that its optimum tracks imbalance severity, yielding a practical, interpretable recipe for ordinal-aware training. Future work includes multi-seed confirmation, direct comparison with dedicated ordinal-regression heads and label-distribution learning, and extension to further grading scales.
